\section{Introduction}

The paradigm of Large Language Model (LLM) training has shifted from Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) \cite{ouyang2022traininglanguagemodelsfollow} to Reinforcement Learning (RL) \cite{Fran_ois_Lavet_2018} alignment. While models such as GPT-4 \cite{Achiam2023GPT4TR} and Claude \cite{TheC3} demonstrate strong reasoning, reliance on dedicated reward models imposes computational bottlenecks. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) \cite{Guo_2025,shao2024deepseekmathpushinglimitsmathematical} eliminates the critic network by normalizing rewards across rollout groups, enabling policies to optimize reasoning paths without separate reward models.

However, on complex multi-step reasoning, vanilla GRPO faces three critical limitations:
(1)~\textbf{Zero Advantage:} On difficult or saturated prompts, rollouts often share identical outcomes. Reward variance vanishes ($\sigma \to 0$), driving advantage values to zero \cite{zhang2026trainlesslearnmore} and stalling updates.
(2)~\textbf{The Compulsive Correction Fallacy:} Multi-layer \cite{ding2025multilayergrpoenhancingreasoning} and iterative reflection \cite{madaan2023selfrefineiterativerefinementselffeedback} induce overcorrection, biasing models to alter valid steps and causing high false-negative drift.
(3)~\textbf{The Noise-Signal Dichotomy:} Standard RL updates treat all failures equally, failing to distinguish stochastic calculation slips from systematic logical flaws.

To overcome these challenges, we propose Semantic-Balanced GRPO (SB-GRPO). We introduce a differentiable Heuristic Confidence Gate to activate Advantage Calibration \cite{nan2025ngrponegativeenhancedgrouprelative} with virtual rewards for uniform batches, sustaining gradient flow. For mixed batches, Semantic Error Profiling (SEP) clusters reasoning paths with frozen MiniLM embeddings to isolate systematic errors, forming Static Balanced Batches modulated by an entropy-aware loss scaling factor.
